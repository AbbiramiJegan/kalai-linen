How to Wash Linen Without Ruining It

Words by KALAI Editorial · Fact-checked by Amara Devaraj, Master Tailor · 4 min read

Most linen doesn't get ruined by wear. It gets ruined by the washing machine — specifically, by heat, harsh agitation, and a tumble dryer set on high. Fix those three things and linen will comfortably outlast almost anything else in your wardrobe.

Wash cool, wash gentle

Set your machine to a cool or lukewarm cycle — around 30°C — on a gentle or delicate setting. Hot water is the single biggest cause of shrinkage and fibre stress in linen. If a garment is genuinely stained, cool water with a longer soak works better than hot water with a short one.

Use a mild, plant-based detergent and skip the fabric softener entirely. Softener coats the fibre in a waxy residue that actually reduces linen's natural breathability over time — the opposite of what you bought it for.

Don't overload the machine

Linen needs room to move freely in the wash. A packed drum means more friction between garments, which is what causes pilling and thread stress at the seams. Wash linen in a smaller, looser load, and separate dark colours from light — linen dye can bleed more than you'd expect in the first few washes.

Skip the tumble dryer

Air drying is non-negotiable if you want your linen to last. Tumble drying on high heat is where most of the damage — shrinkage, thread brittleness, that papery feel — actually happens. Hang garments on a padded hanger or lay them flat, ideally out of direct sun to protect colour.

Ironing (if you want to)

Linen's wrinkles are part of its character, not a flaw — but if you prefer it smooth, iron while the fabric is still slightly damp, on a medium-high heat setting. A pressing cloth or the reverse side protects any darker colours from heat shine.

Storage that keeps its shape

Store linen loosely folded or on a hanger with some breathing room — never compressed in a vacuum bag, which crushes the fibre over time. A breathable cotton garment bag protects against dust while letting the fabric air out between wears.

  • Do: wash cool, use mild detergent, air dry, iron slightly damp
  • Don't: use hot water, fabric softener, high-heat tumble drying, or vacuum storage

“Most linen doesn't get ruined by wear. It gets ruined by the washing machine.”
