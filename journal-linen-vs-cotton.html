Linen vs Cotton: Which Is Cooler?

Words by KALAI Editorial · Fact-checked by Amara Devaraj, Master Tailor · 5 min read

The short answer: linen is the cooler fabric in real heat, and it isn't close. The reason has less to do with the plant it comes from and more to do with how each fibre is built.

Cotton fibres are short and twist into a relatively dense, springy yarn. That density is great for softness, but it traps a layer of warm air close to the skin — the same quality that makes a cotton t-shirt feel cosy on a cool morning works against you at 32°C.

Why linen breathes differently

Linen comes from the flax plant's stem, not a seed pod, which gives it a long, straight fibre with a natural hollow core. That hollow structure does two things at once: it lets air move directly through the weave instead of sitting trapped against your skin, and it allows the fibre to absorb and release moisture quickly — up to 20% of its own weight in water before it even feels damp to the touch.

In practice, that means linen pulls sweat away from your body and lets it evaporate fast, which is the actual mechanism your body uses to cool itself. A fabric that gets out of the way of evaporation will always feel cooler than one that holds moisture against the skin.

Where cotton still wins

We'd rather tell you the honest trade-offs than oversell linen. Cotton drapes softer straight out of the wash, wrinkles less, and for babies or very sensitive skin, a fine combed cotton can feel gentler against the body than a heavier linen weave. If you want a fabric that looks crisp all day without any ironing, cotton — or a linen-cotton blend — is the more forgiving choice.

What actually matters: weight, not just fibre

A heavy 260 GSM linen can feel hotter than a fine 120 GSM cotton voile. Fibre is only half the story — weave and weight decide the rest. For genuine tropical heat, look for a lightweight, open-weave linen between 130–170 GSM. That's the range we use for most of The Kalai Edit, including the shirts below.

  • Choose linen for: peak heat, humidity, all-day wear, pieces you'll rewear often
  • Choose cotton for: low-iron convenience, babies and sensitive skin, structured tailoring
  • Choose a blend for: a middle ground — less wrinkling, most of the breathability

“A fabric that gets out of the way of evaporation will always feel cooler than one that holds moisture against the skin.”
